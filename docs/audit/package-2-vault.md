# Audit package 2: shielded vault and circuits

The shielded vault holds admitted ERC-20s against private notes. It goes live on mainnet after three things: this contract audit, a separate ZK audit of the circuits, and a public multi-party trusted setup ceremony whose verifiers are deployed in a new pool.

- **Commit:** tag `audit-2-vault`.
- **Spec:** [`docs/vault-spec.md`](../vault-spec.md) fixes the construction. The circuits, the contract, the client library (`packages/vault-core`) and the services implement it together.
- **Contract toolchain:** as package 1 (solc 0.8.28, OpenZeppelin 5.6.1).
- **Circuit toolchain:** circom 2.2.3 (npm `circom2` 0.2.23), circomlib 2.0.5, snarkjs 0.7.6, Groth16 over BN254, Poseidon with circomlib parameters.
- **Depends on:** package 1 (AssetRegistry, CurveilRouter, DAMM and DLMM pools, which the adapters call).

## 2. Construction in one page

```
ownerHash  = Poseidon2(ownerKey, blinding)                       client
commitment = Poseidon4(resource, amount, ownerHash, noteDomain)  contract (shield) or circuit
nullifier  = Poseidon3(spendingKey, commitment, leafIndex)       circuit only
noteDomain = keccak(abi.encode(tag, chainId, pool)) mod p        binds notes to one chain and pool
context    = keccak(everything the contract checks outside the circuit) mod p
```

- **Shield** needs no proof. The contract measures the amount received, builds the commitment and inserts it.
- **Transfer** proves two inputs and creates two outputs. The fee is paid in the spent asset (`fee · (feeResource − resource) = 0`).
- **Withdraw** proves two inputs, pays `amount − protocolFee` to the recipient and `fee` to the fee recipient, and creates a change note. The protocol fee rate is in the context, so a rate change invalidates older proofs instead of charging more.
- **Execute** spends up to two legs (each a withdraw proof under the execute context) into an approved adapter, and turns its outputs into notes with amounts set on chain, or into public payouts. The execute context commits to every leg, output, fee, route and deadline.
- The circuit binds `context` by constraining `context · context`. Amounts are range-checked to 128 bits in the circuit and bounded by `MAX_AMOUNT = 2^128 − 1` in the contract.

## 3. Privileged roles

On mainnet the owner is the timelock (72 hours) and the guardian is the Safe.

| Role | Can | Cannot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| owner | `configureAsset` (shielding switch, per-deposit cap), `setWithdrawFeeBps` (≤ 1%), `setProtocolFeeRecipient`, `setAdapter` (Active or ExitOnly), `setGuardian`, pause, unpause | Change verifiers (immutable), spend or move notes, block exits from an asset by switching shielding off, fully disable an adapter once enabled |
| guardian | Pause shielding (until the owner lifts it) and pause spending (at most 72 hours, see below) | Unpause, extend a spending pause, start one within 24 hours of the last |
| anyone | `claimProtocolFees(token)`, which pays only `protocolFeeRecipient` | |
| adapter | Receive released tokens for one action and return outputs | Touch notes; outputs are checked against the pool's balance after settling |

**Spending pause.** A spending pause stops transfers, withdrawals and executions, so it is limited: it ends on its own after `MAX_SPEND_PAUSE = 72 hours`, calling `pause` again does not extend it, and a new one cannot start until `SPEND_PAUSE_GAP = 24 hours` after the last one ended, even if the owner ended it early. So no key holder, honest or compromised, can trap funds for more than 72 hours in any 96. [`ShieldedPool.t.sol test_SpendPauseExpiresAndCannotBeChained`, `test_EarlyUnpauseStartsTheGap`]

## 4. Trust assumptions

1. **Trusted setup.** Groth16 soundness depends on at least one honest ceremony participant. Testnet uses a single-party setup labelled as such. Mainnet uses a public phase-1 transcript (a 2^16 Powers of Tau or larger) and a phase-2 ceremony per circuit with at least 5 independent contributors and a public random beacon. The resulting verifiers ship as a new pool.
2. **Poseidon and BN254** as in circomlib. The contract's on-chain Poseidon (`poseidon-solidity`) must match circomlib's parameters exactly; tests check both against the same vectors.
3. **Adapters are trusted with what is sent to them** for the length of one call. Each is audited. New adapters start ExitOnly-capable.
4. **USDG and WETH** (see package 1). A frozen pool address would stop withdrawals of that asset.
5. **The broadcaster** sees users' network addresses and timing. It cannot change recipients, amounts or fees. Users can always submit from their own wallet.
6. **Privacy depends on the anonymity set.** Deposits and withdrawals are public. Small or unusual amounts can be linked by amount and timing. The app warns about this.

## 5. Invariants

- Every token the pool holds is backed: balance = notes outstanding + protocol fees accrued + donations. [`VaultInvariant.invariant_backed`]
- Nothing leaves beyond what was deposited. [`invariant_nothingLeavesBeyondDeposits`]
- No nullifier is spent twice, and two legs or inputs never share a nullifier. [`invariant_noDoubleSpend`, `ShieldedPool.t.sol`]
- A proof is bound to its chain, pool, recipients, fees, fee rate, deadline and ciphertexts: changing any one fails verification. [`ShieldedPool.t.sol`, `ShieldedExecute.t.sol`]
- A zero-fee transfer has one canonical form (`feeResource = 0`, `feeRecipient = 0`).
- Switching shielding off for an asset never blocks its exits. An adapter, once enabled, can always be exited.
- After every execute, the pool's balance of every output token covers what it owes. [`ShieldedExecute.t.sol`]
- In the circuits: sum of inputs = sum of outputs + public out; each input is in the tree under `root` or has amount 0; nullifiers are recomputed from the spending key; the two nullifiers differ; every amount fits in 128 bits.

## 6. Known issues and accepted risks

1. **Position ids can be front-run.** The client picks a random `positionId` for a liquidity entry. Anyone who sees the pending entry can open the same id first. That only makes the entry revert (no funds at risk), and the client retries with a new id.
2. **Shares move only without a fee.** A broadcaster fee would have to be paid in shares, so share transfers are self-submitted.
3. **Public parts of execution.** The market action, amounts, position and any public payout are public by design. Only the link to the account is hidden.
4. **Verifiers are immutable.** A circuit fix ships as a new pool. The old pool keeps honouring exits.
5. **Root history of 100.** A proof made against an older root fails and must be rebuilt. At testnet volumes this is days; the client rebuilds automatically.

## 7. Static analysis

- **Slither** on the contracts: `incorrect-return` in the generated verifiers (assembly `return` by design, false positive); `reentrancy-balance` in `ShieldedLiquidityAdapter` (calls only factory pools, and is only reachable from the pool's `nonReentrant` `execute`, false positive); `incorrect-equality` on zero checks (false positive).
- **circomspect 0.9.0** on both circuits: 7 notes each. Six are loop-counter arithmetic and comparisons on compile-time variables. One warns that `contextSquare` appears in a single constraint, which is the intended way to bind a public input. No other findings.

## 8. Tests and coverage

`ShieldedPool.t.sol` (18 tests), `ShieldedExecute.t.sol` (25 tests, real Groth16 proofs from the testnet artifacts), `VaultInvariant.t.sol` (3 invariants, verifier mocked so the handler can explore spends freely), `packages/circuits/test` (artifact hashes match the setup record and the manifest), and `packages/vault-core/test/circuits.test.ts` (real proofs from the client library verified against the keys, including rejected witnesses).

Line coverage reported by `forge coverage` understates the vault: the execute suite uses a proof fixture tied to exact contract addresses, and coverage builds without the optimizer, which changes them, so that suite is excluded from the coverage run. The adapters are exercised only by that suite. See [`coverage-package-1.txt`](coverage-package-1.txt) for the numbers and the exclusion.

## 9. Where to look hardest

1. **Circuit soundness:** can a witness satisfy the constraints with an input that is not in the tree, a reused nullifier, or amounts that wrap the field?
2. **Context binding:** is every value the contract uses outside the proof inside `context`, for all three actions?
3. **Execute settlement** (`_spendLeg`, `_settleOutput`, `_checkBacking`): output order, fees on token and share outputs, the zero-net case, and whether an adapter can return less than it reports.
4. **Share resources:** can a share of one adapter or position ever be spent as another, or as a token?
5. **Liquidity adapter exits:** pro-rata maths across partial exits, fees claimed before exit, and the last exit taking everything.
6. **Pause rules:** the 72-hour cap and 24-hour gap under every ordering of pause, unpause and expiry.
